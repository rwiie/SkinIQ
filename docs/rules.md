# Knowledge-base rules (proposal 3.6.3)

Generated by `python tools/make_rules_doc.py` from `skiniq/rules/`. Every rule has a test in `tests/test_rules.py`, and every rule records its source.

**Actions:** *exclude* removes the product; *caution* keeps it with a warning and a score penalty; *prefer* keeps it with a reason and a score bonus. Penalties and bonuses add up to each product's rule score, which the ranking (3.6.4) combines with TF-IDF similarity.

Hard exclusions are used only for safety (pregnancy, ingredients the user avoids, medical skin conditions, harsh combinations for sensitive skin). The product dataset's `afterUse` tags come from an ingredient checker and fire on single ingredients (37% of products are tagged "Acne Trigger"), so they are used only as cautions or preferences.

## Routine-level compatibility checks

`check_routine()` flags pairs of products in one routine: retinoid + AHA/BHA and two exfoliating products (Poelhekken & van der Bent, 2026), and hydroquinone + retinoid (Kim et al., 2017).

Removed for weak evidence: vitamin C + AHA/BHA in one product, and vitamin C + retinoid in one routine.

### New references (add to the report's reference list)

- Bozzo, P., Chua-Gocheco, A., & Einarson, A. (2011). Safety of skin care products during pregnancy. *Canadian Family Physician, 57*(6), 665–667. — Hydroquinone is substantially absorbed through the skin and best avoided in pregnancy; topical retinoids are advised against (case reports of birth defects with topical tretinoin).
- Kaplan, Y. C., Ozsarfati, J., Etwel, F., Nickel, C., Nulman, I., & Koren, G. (2015). Pregnancy outcomes following first-trimester exposure to topical retinoids: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *British Journal of Dermatology, 173*(5), 1132–1141. — No significant rise in major malformations, but statistical power too low to justify use in pregnancy, so avoidance remains the precaution. **Verify the author list and pages on PubMed (PMID 26215715) before citing.**
- U.S. Food and Drug Administration. (2005). *Guidance for industry: Labeling for topically applied cosmetic products containing alpha hydroxy acids as ingredients.* — AHAs increased UV sensitivity (sunburn) by about 18% after four weeks; recommends sunscreen during use and for a week after.
- World Health Organization. (2002). *Global solar UV index: A practical guide.* WHO. — UV index categories: low 0–2, moderate 3–5, high 6–7, very high 8–10, extreme 11+. **Check these bands against the WHO PDF before citing.**

The sun-sensitivity caution for retinoids follows the same FDA reasoning for AHAs; for a retinoid-specific source, check Bozzo et al. (2011) or a dermatology textbook.
